I’ve never seen a startup scale that wasn't customer-centric at its core. In a 0–1 environment, that manifests as over-servicing your first users and turning their opinions into features. The advantage here is proximity. When PMs have direct and unmediated relationships with users, the feedback loop is high-fidelity and unfiltered.

At scale, access often gets filtered through layers of process and "protection."

Most companies treat customer centricity as a moral virtue; it isn't. It is a capital allocation strategy. High-performing teams know that every line of code - every hour of build time -- is an investment. We've all seen what the gamble looks like: Product-centric teams build what the "trio" finds elegant, while Sales-centric teams build what the last prospect demanded. Both create noise. 

True customer centricity is the courage to say "No" to the noise because it distracts from the long-term outcome. It is the marriage of qualitative intuition and quantitative evidence. When the cycle that takes instinct and drives measured outcomes shortens, capital efficiency increases and margins expand.


# The Four Stages of Customer Centricity

![customer-centricity-stages]({{site.url}}/assets/images/posts/product/customer-centricity/customer-centricity-stages.jpeg)

## Level 0: The feature factory:
At this stage, there's a "Feature Factory." The roadmap is a disorganized reaction to the loudest exec or the latest competitor announcement. The risk is a critically high opportunity cost. The team spends expensive engineering hours building things that nobody uses. At this stage, as a PM, you're looking at a map of your competitors, not the actual ground your users walk on.


## Level 1: Order taking:
The team has started to listen but are simply taking orders. The roadmap solves for present-day challenges, which are often just symptoms of a root issue. Ad-hoc interviews are taken and though it feels good, it's inconsistent and often inaccessible. Like a parent who gives a child candy because they are crying—the immediate noise has been solved but there's been no improvement to their overall health. As a parent, sometimes this has to happen, but you're conscious of the risks.


## Level 2: Systems thinking:
Achieving product-market-fit (PMF) is no longer a guessing game, but a process and system that's being executed. The underlying jobs to be done are understood, and importantly, users' frustrations and motivations are embodied. Teams anticipate friction in the workflow before they're mentioned and regularly deploy second-order thinking. The product delivers lower churn and faster revenue expansion. The team feels a "soulful" connection to the user's struggle.


## Level 3: Category leadership:
The team knows its customer's future needs better than tehy do becase they see the macro-shifts in the industry, and have a pointed view of their role. Teams aren't just responding to the market -- they're defining it. The team quickly moves from being a 'vendor' to being a 'partner' and customers aren't just purhcasing software and features -- they're betting their careres on the team's roadmap.

# Customer centricity is measured across 5 dimensions:
No maturity model is complete without the underlying skills, and here I share a system of evaluation.

1. **Signal**: Where is signal coming from, and how often? Where is it concentrated -- where does it stop propoagating? Is the signal relayed from sales or research reports -- derivative signal -- or do team shave direct and unmediated access to their users? 

2. **Interpretation**: How does the team translate raw pain into a measurable outcome? Does an observation (e.g. more users) become the measure (i.e. is it easy to game?) - Goodhart's law. Are there clear counter-balance metrics? Teams move from 'what they said' to 'what it costs them'. 

3. **Allocation**: How is capital deployed? Are you funding outcomes or hypotheses? Are you invested in 10-mo development cycles or shorter proof points? Is the discipline to prove it and scale it taken?

4. **Build**: What does the build path and pipeline look like? What is the cylce time between insight to build? Are all builds shipped with clear instrumentation? Teams often build the feature, but miss the telemetry that proves that their investment is working. 

5. **Measure**: Is the impact validated and then learning fed into the system? Good teams measure to celebrate - we all do this. Great teams measure to seek honesty - postiive or failure. They use the measure and it impacts their roadmap and your team is not afraid to shift as they learn.

# Testing for customer-centricity
Truly customer-centric teams have these examples at hand:

1. They can name the single customer outcome you are allocating against this quarter.
2. They can show the metric that moves when you ship.
3. They can explain the most valuable feature that was declined, and why.


# Conclusion
Customer centricity isn't kindness, nor is it a moral virtue. It's discipline. It's the courage to prioritize the future customer over today's request. And when you get it right, the flywheel doesn't just spin. It compounds. Instead of reacting to the market, teams define it and lead their customers to the 'promise land'.
